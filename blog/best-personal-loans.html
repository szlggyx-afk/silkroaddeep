Best Personal Loans in 2026: Compare Top Lenders Side by Side

By CalcWise Team · August 1, 2026 · 10 min read

Personal loans are one of the most versatile financial tools available. Whether you're consolidating high-interest credit card debt, funding a home improvement project, covering a medical expense, or planning a wedding, a personal loan can provide the funds you need with a fixed repayment schedule and predictable monthly payments.

But with dozens of lenders competing for your business, finding the best personal loan can be overwhelming. We've compared the top lenders in 2026 across key metrics—interest rates, loan terms, fees, credit requirements, and borrower experience—to help you find the right fit for your needs.

Detailed Lender Reviews

1. SoFi — Best Overall for Good Credit

SoFi is consistently one of the top-rated personal loan lenders, and for good reason. It offers competitive rates starting at 5.99% APR, loan amounts up to $100,000, and charges no origination fees, no late fees, and no prepayment penalties. SoFi also provides unique borrower perks like unemployment protection (which can pause your payments if you lose your job) and free financial advising.

Best for: Borrowers with good to excellent credit (680+) who want a fee-free loan with member benefits.

Drawbacks: Minimum credit score requirement is higher than some competitors, and you'll need a solid credit history to qualify for the lowest rates.

2. LightStream — Best for Excellent Credit

A division of Truist Bank, LightStream offers some of the lowest personal loan rates available, starting at 5.99% APR with autopay. It stands out for its "Rate Beat" program—if you find a lower rate from another lender, LightStream will beat it by 0.10 percentage points. Loan terms extend up to 12 years for certain purposes, and funding can happen as soon as the same day.

Best for: Borrowers with excellent credit (720+) who want the lowest possible rate and flexible terms.

Drawbacks: No pre-qualification option (you'll need to submit a formal application to see your rate), and higher rates for borrowers with average credit.

3. Upstart — Best for Fair Credit

Upstart uses artificial intelligence and non-traditional data (like education and employment history) to evaluate borrowers, making it a good option for those with limited credit history or fair credit scores. While its APR range goes up to 35.99%, many borrowers with thin credit files qualify for better rates than they'd get elsewhere. Loan amounts start as low as $1,000.

Best for: Borrowers with fair credit (600–680) or limited credit history who may not qualify with traditional lenders.

Drawbacks: Origination fees of 0%–12% may apply, and the maximum loan amount is $50,000.

4. Discover — Best for Debt Consolidation

Discover personal loans are a solid choice for debt consolidation, with direct payment to creditors available. Rates start at 6.99% APR, and there are no origination fees, no closing costs, and no prepayment penalties. Discover also offers a 30-day money-back guarantee—if you change your mind within 30 days, you can return the loan funds with no interest.

Best for: Borrowers consolidating credit card debt who want a trusted, well-known lender with no fees.

Drawbacks: Maximum loan amount is $40,000, and you'll need a credit score of at least 660 to qualify.

5. Marcus by Goldman Sachs — Best for No Fees

Marcus offers personal loans with no fees whatsoever—no origination fee, no late fee, no prepayment penalty. It also offers a unique "On-Time Payment Reward": if you make 12 consecutive on-time payments, you can defer one payment without accruing interest. Rates start at 6.99% APR, and you can choose your monthly payment date.

Best for: Borrowers who want a straightforward, fee-free loan with flexible payment options.

Drawbacks: Maximum loan amount is $40,000, and co-signers are not allowed.

6. Best Egg — Best for Quick Funding

Best Egg specializes in fast personal loans, with funding possible as soon as the next business day. It offers loan amounts from $2,000 to $50,000 with terms of 3 or 5 years. Best Egg also has a secured loan option for borrowers who want to use their home equity to secure a lower rate.

Best for: Borrowers who need funds quickly and have decent credit (640+).

Drawbacks: Origination fees of 0.99%–5.99%, and limited term options.

7. LendingClub — Best for All Credit Types

LendingClub is a peer-to-peer lending platform that connects borrowers with investors. It accepts borrowers across a wide range of credit profiles, making it more accessible than many traditional lenders. Joint applications are allowed, which can help borrowers with lower credit qualify for better rates.

Best for: Borrowers with average or below-average credit who want to apply with a co-borrower.

Drawbacks: Origination fees of 2%–6%, and APRs can be high for lower-credit borrowers (up to 36%).

How to Choose the Best Personal Loan

1. Check Your Credit Score

Your credit score is the most important factor in determining your interest rate. Get a free copy of your credit report from AnnualCreditReport.com and check your score. Knowing where you stand will help you identify which lenders you're likely to qualify with.

2. Get Pre-Qualified with Multiple Lenders

Most online lenders offer pre-qualification with a soft credit check that doesn't affect your score. Get pre-qualified with at least 3–5 lenders to compare rates and terms. This is the single most effective way to ensure you're getting the best deal.

3. Compare APR, Not Just Interest Rate

The annual percentage rate (APR) includes both the interest rate and any fees (like origination fees), giving you a more accurate picture of the loan's total cost. A loan with a lower interest rate but high fees may actually cost more than a loan with a slightly higher rate and no fees.

4. Consider the Total Cost, Not Just the Monthly Payment

A longer loan term means lower monthly payments, but you'll pay more in total interest. Use our loan calculator to compare the total cost of different loan terms and find the right balance between affordable payments and minimizing interest.

5. Watch for Prepayment Penalties

Some lenders charge a fee if you pay off your loan early. All of the lenders listed above do not charge prepayment penalties, but it's always worth confirming before you sign.

When a Personal Loan Makes Sense

  • Debt consolidation — Combining high-interest credit card debt into a single lower-interest loan can save you money and simplify payments.
  • Home improvements — A personal loan can be a good alternative to a HELOC, especially if you don't have much equity or don't want to use your home as collateral.
  • Emergency expenses — For unexpected medical bills or car repairs, a personal loan is often cheaper than credit cards.
  • Major purchases — Financing a large purchase with a personal loan may be cheaper than store financing or credit cards.

When to Avoid a Personal Loan

  • You can't afford the payments — Never borrow more than you can comfortably repay.
  • For discretionary spending — Financing a vacation or luxury purchase with a personal loan is rarely a good idea.
  • You qualify for a 0% credit card — If you can pay off the balance during the 0% intro period, a credit card may be cheaper.
  • You're already struggling with debt — Taking on more debt to pay existing debt can create a cycle. Consider credit counseling instead.

Bottom Line

The best personal loan for you depends on your credit profile, borrowing needs, and financial goals. For borrowers with good credit, SoFi and LightStream offer the lowest rates and best terms. If your credit is fair, Upstart may approve you when others won't. And for debt consolidation, Discover's direct payment feature makes the process seamless.

Regardless of which lender you choose, always compare multiple offers and read the fine print. And use our loan calculator to understand exactly what your monthly payment and total cost will be before you commit.
